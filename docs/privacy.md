# Privacy

This page says what impossibleos.co collects when you visit it, and who collects it. The site itself has no accounts, no forms and no analytics of its own. What follows comes from the services it is hosted on or loads.

## Who runs this site?

Impossible OS is published by Rizonetech (Pty) Ltd. Questions about this page go to the [project's issue tracker](https://github.com/rizonesoft/impossible-os/issues).

## Which services see my visit?

- **GitHub Pages** hosts the site and receives every request, including your IP address, as its server logs. See the [GitHub General Privacy Statement](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement).
- **Google Fonts** serves the typefaces, so your browser sends Google a request for them. See the [Google Fonts privacy FAQ](https://developers.google.com/fonts/faq/privacy).
- **Google AdSense** shows advertising on the documentation pages. The landing page, the design pages and the error pages carry no ads.
- **jsDelivr** serves the diagram library to documentation pages that contain a diagram.
- **hits.sh** draws the visitor counter at the foot of the landing page and counts the request.
- **PayPal** receives you only if you choose the Donate link.

## How does advertising use cookies?

Google and its partners use cookies to serve ads based on your earlier visits to this site and to other sites. You can turn personalised advertising off in [Google's My Ad Center](https://myadcenter.google.com/), and read [how Google uses information from sites that use its services](https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites). Ads may also be served by third-party vendors and ad networks that Google certifies; they are listed in [Google's ad technology providers](https://support.google.com/admanager/answer/9012903), and they use cookies the same way. You can opt out of interest-based advertising from many of these vendors at [aboutads.info](https://www.aboutads.info/choices/); they may still use cookies for other purposes, such as measurement and fraud prevention.

## What happens in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland?

Visitors there are asked for consent by Google's own consent message, a certified consent management platform, before personalised ads are served. If you allow storage but decline personalisation, Google may show non-personalised ads, which are based on the page you are reading rather than your browsing history; they still use cookies for frequency capping, fraud prevention and reporting. If you refuse storage altogether, Google shows limited ads, which use no cookies for advertising but may still store an identifier for fraud detection, or no ad at all.

## Does the site store anything in my browser?

The documentation pages remember your light or dark theme choice in your browser's local storage. It is never sent anywhere.
